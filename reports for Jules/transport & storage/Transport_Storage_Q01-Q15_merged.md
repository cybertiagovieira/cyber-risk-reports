*Source file: File A Part 1 (Section C) and Section A; File B Section A (sector definition). Conclusion: TOS, WMS, TMS, DCS and SCADA/ICS platforms are single points of failure with low substitutability in File A's assessment; File B adds that Section H is heterogeneous and equal operating risk across modes must not be inferred.*

**Operating model (File A)**
- [S-001] Logistics and transport business models generate revenue through cargo throughput, freight ton-miles or passenger-distance metrics. Operational processes rely on uninterrupted data flows across enterprise software, industrial automation and external service infrastructure.
- [S-002] Operating structures range from centralised corporate networks to decentralised joint-venture terminal operations. Multi-tenant decentralised models provide local operational autonomy but frequently create visibility gaps and unmonitored vendor access points.

**Sector definition (File B)**
- [S-078] For international comparison, ISIC Rev.4 Section H, divisions 49-53: land transport and pipelines; water transport; air transport; warehousing and transport support; postal and courier services (Part Two, Chapter I, p.43; Part Three, Section H, pp.54-55).

**Dependency analysis and cross-sector concentration (File A; DEP-07 to DEP-09)**
- **DEP-07 TOS and WMS.** [S-001] TOS platforms (such as Navis N4) and enterprise WMS act as central execution engines for maritime terminals and distribution facilities; a total outage instantly paralyses automated crane movements, gate check-ins and yard slot assignments. [S-002] Manual fallback ("paper and radio" operations) sustains less than 15% to 20% of standard baseline throughput, leading to gate congestion and facility closure within 24 to 48 hours.
- **DEP-08 ICS and OT.** [S-012] Industrial controllers, PLCs and SCADA networks manage physical transport machinery, including pipeline valves, automated stacker cranes and rail interlockings. [S-001] Because of risk aversion regarding lateral movement from corporate IT into OT, operators frequently execute emergency disconnections, turning an IT network intrusion into an immediate physical transport shutdown.
- **DEP-09 Cloud hosting, cellular telematics and GNSS.** [S-004] Logistics operators rely on continuous cloud connectivity for shipment tracking, cellular networks for driver telematics and GNSS/GPS signals for automated yard crane positioning and vessel navigation. Widespread cloud outages or GNSS spoofing disrupt fleet positioning, cargo manifest validation and automated container handling across entire transport corridors.

2. **DP World Australia (Citrix intrusion 2023).** [S-001] Establishes that emergency network disconnection, implemented defensively to contain an IT perimeter intrusion, creates the same operational disruption as ransomware encryption, halting 40% of national maritime cargo.

**Case Study CS-1: A.P. Moller-Maersk (NotPetya) - INC-01**
- *Selection rationale:* [S-001] Definitive historical landmark demonstrating unmitigated supply-chain lateral propagation and total operational paralysis across global port infrastructure.
- *Timeline:* [S-001] 27 June 2017 (infection via M.E.Doc software update); Days 1-3 (global IT paralysis and terminal suspension); Day 10 (baseline booking capabilities restored); Day 14+ (full network re-architecture completed).
- *Initial access & mechanism:* [S-001] Supply-chain breach of Ukrainian accounting software (M.E.Doc). Petya/NotPetya malware used EternalBlue (CVE-2017-0144) and PsExec to spread across flat network architectures.
- *Exploited weaknesses:* [S-001] Complete absence of network segmentation between regional accounting systems and global terminal operations, with unpatched SMBv1 vulnerabilities.
- *Affected services:* [S-001] APM Terminals global gate operations, online booking engines, tracking portals and internal logistics dispatch engines.
- *Operational & financial effects:* [S-001] 76 container terminals halted. Gate entry closed and container tracking went dark. Disclosed direct financial loss reached $300M, driven by lost freight revenue and emergency manual workarounds.
- *Recovery constraints:* [S-001] Required deploying 45,000 end-user PCs and 4,000 servers from scratch. Global Active Directory domain controllers had to be restored from an isolated offline domain controller in Ghana.
- *Regulatory outcome:* [S-001] Accelerated global maritime cybersecurity mandates and inspired international port security frameworks.
- *Lessons:* [S-001] Flat network architectures allow localised supplier breaches to become catastrophic operational events.
- *What the case does not establish:* [S-001] Targeted intent against maritime logistics; Maersk was collateral damage in a state-sponsored regional cyber campaign.

**Case Study CS-2: DP World Australia - INC-04**
- *Selection rationale:* [S-001] Emergency network isolation deployed defensively during an IT intrusion causes immediate physical transport paralysis.
- *Timeline:* [S-001] 10 November 2023 (unauthorised access detected); 10 November 2023 (proactive internet and TOS disconnection); 13 November 2023 (port gates partially reopened); 20 November 2023 (backlog clearance under way).
- *Initial access & mechanism:* [S-005] Exploitation of unpatched remote access equipment (Citrix Bleed, CVE-2023-4966), allowing threat actors to bypass MFA.
- *Exploited weaknesses:* [S-005] Delayed patch implementation on perimeter edge devices and unmonitored administrative session tokens.
- *Affected services:* [S-001] Container gate handling, stevedoring crane dispatch and yard management across Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane and Fremantle terminals.
- *Operational & financial effects:* [S-001] 30,000 shipping containers stranded at quay walls. Australia's maritime freight capacity dropped by 40% for 4 days, incurring substantial overtime labour costs and port congestion.
- *Recovery constraints:* [S-005] 696 hours of forensic investigation and system validation before reconnecting TOS gateways to the public internet.
- *Regulatory outcome:* [S-027] Direct supervisory intervention by the Australian National Cyber Security Coordinator and Home Affairs Department.
- *Lessons:* [S-001] Proactive network disconnection acts as a self-inflicted operational denial of service unless isolated, out-of-band TOS operating modes exist.
- *What the case does not establish:* [S-001] That ransomware payloads encrypted OT; disruption resulted entirely from containment disconnections.

**Case Study CS-3: Transport for London - INC-06**
- *Selection rationale:* [S-006] Vulnerability to social engineering targeting helpdesk personnel and long-tail liabilities of legacy database exposure.
- *Timeline:* [S-006] 31 August 2024 (initial unauthorised access); 1 September 2024 (suspicious activity detected); 12 September 2024 (public disclosure); 16 September 2024 (arrest of two suspects); February 2025 (UK ICO closes regulatory inquiry).
- *Initial access & mechanism:* [S-006] Social engineering (vishing / helpdesk impersonation) by threat actors linked to Scattered Spider, gaining legitimate user access.
- *Exploited weaknesses:* [S-006] Weak helpdesk identity-check protocols, lack of zero-trust network controls and legacy server databases storing unencrypted historical customer refund records.
- *Affected services:* [S-006] Oyster card online refund portals, live travel information feeds and internal administrative networks.
- *Operational & financial effects:* [S-006] Live feeds taken offline. [S-022] All 28,000 TfL employees required in-person attendance at corporate headquarters for forced password resets. [S-006] Personal information for 10 million customers compromised; £39M in direct remediation outlays.
- *Recovery constraints:* [S-022] Rebuilding identity trust required physically re-authenticating 28,000 employee credentials across corporate offices.
- *Regulatory outcome:* [S-025] UK ICO cleared TfL from formal monetary penalties due to swift containment, but mandated strict legacy database purging and identity-check enhancements.

### Q09.0 File A executive summary for Senior Credit Officers (preserved)
- [S-001] Cyber risk in the Transport & Storage sector has transformed from an operational inconvenience into a key cash-flow and credit risk driver. Modern transport business models depend on interconnected digital management networks - including TOS, WMS and automated fleet telematics - that operate with minimal inventory buffers.
- Three primary exposures: (1) [S-001] OT and management system dependency: system intrusions or defensive network isolations rapidly paralyse cargo handling, stranding freight and stopping revenue generation; (2) [S-004] supply-chain and legacy vulnerability: compromised vendor access and unpatched edge devices allow threat actors to bypass perimeters and breach core logistics management infrastructure; (3) [S-007] rigorous regulatory liabilities: compliance frameworks, including EU NIS2, US Coast Guard 33 CFR mandates and UK regulations, impose strict 24-hour reporting timelines, severe fines and potential executive management liability for non-compliance (see conflict C-18).

### Q10.1 Typology, materiality and overlap allocation (File B D1)
