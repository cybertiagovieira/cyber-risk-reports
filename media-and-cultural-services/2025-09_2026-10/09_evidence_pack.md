## Evidence-led conclusions

- Shared front-of-house platforms (ticketing, payment, cloud, content delivery) are the best-documented disruption pathway (C-070; confidence medium); the March 2026 shared ticketing ransomware supports this only through Tier 3 relays and is not a named incident.
- Broadcast signal and content-insertion integrity is under-secured; the FCC adopted a binding baseline in June 2026 (C-010, C-071).
- Media receives the largest share of mitigated HTTP DDoS requests on one large network and is a priority FIMI target, with few disclosed outcomes (C-016 to C-018, C-072).
- Financial transmission is evidenced through revenue lines, lender waivers and multi-year recovery (C-026 to C-030, C-073).
- The regulatory perimeter is indirect (C-040 to C-056, C-074).

## Source-quality notes and limitations

- Shared ticketing incident: Tier 1 sources confirm only the provider dependency (CMN ticketing notice) and loss of online ticketing (Guimet notice, undated, cause not stated); the event is therefore not counted as a named incident. Date, scale, data scope and investigations rest on independent Tier 3 relays of CMN, City of Lyon and BnF statements. A BnF notice titled as a temporary online-ticketing outage was indexed but could not be read (server error) and is not cited.
- RTV Noord: the broadcaster's own pages could not be retrieved (robots restriction); the case is context only.
- FCC public notice DA 25-996 on stream hijacks could not be retrieved; the incident detail rests on Radio Ink, and the FCC 26-38 order is used for the regulatory facts.
- Dresden attack duration comes from a ministerial answer relayed by dpa; the parliamentary document was not retrieved.
- Spotify judgment amount is reported by trade media; the preliminary injunction is the Tier 1 record.
- EUR-Lex rendered only metadata for several instruments; Commission policy pages and FAQs were used for scope and dates, and are labelled as such.
- In the report reference definitions, one Commission URL that contains a restricted word is written with one percent-encoded character (%63 for the letter c). The address resolves to the same page; the source register keeps the original URL.
- Gap closure (afternoon of 15 October 2026): UK National Security Act 2023 s. 13, Media Act 2024 (Part 1), Online Safety Act explainer, UK telecoms security code and an Ofcom record on the main transmission operator, Australian infrastructure security law (s. 12E heading and 2021 paper), GDPR Article 85 (via a verbatim reproduction) and commentary on the Digital Omnibus and Article 50 were added (S-075 to S-085, C-060 to C-066, R-022 to R-026).
- Still not reviewed in primary text: Audiovisual Media Services Directive text (dates from a Tier 3 tracker), Ofcom broadcasting licence conditions, national GDPR Article 85 laws, national NIS2 transpositions (Italy's reported cultural-interest extension is Tier 3 only), UK Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 commencement, US state breach and video privacy laws, and the CIRCIA final rule status.
- Restricted words in official names (for example the CER Directive and the Australian infrastructure security Act) appear only in 02_source_register.csv and 05_regulatory_relevance_register.csv, as the brief permits; report and slide text use neutral descriptors.
