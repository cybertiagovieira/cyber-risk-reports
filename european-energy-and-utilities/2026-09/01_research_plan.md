# European Energy and Utilities - September 2026 Research Plan

## Decision Questions

1. Which September 2026 threat and dependency pathways create the highest operational and systemic exposure for European energy and utilities?
2. Which public incidents, exploitation patterns and regulatory developments changed the sector risk outlook during the reporting period?
3. Which sector-informed actions and external indicators should management prioritise over the next 12 months?

## Evidence Modules

| Module | Intended deck use | Evidence priority |
|---|---|---|
| Sector outlook and macro context | Slide 1 | Official energy and resilience bodies, sector associations, transparent research. |
| Threat activity and exploited technologies | Slide 2 | National cyber authorities, ENISA, CISA KEV, law enforcement and victim disclosures. |
| Public financial and operational impact | Slide 3 | Victim disclosures, public financial reporting, official incident investigations and regulator findings. |
| Scenario pathways | Slide 4 | Public dependency evidence, case evidence and stated assumptions. |
| Colonial Pipeline case study | Slide 5 | Official US government, Congressional and victim sources. |
| Systemic dependencies and regulation | Slide 6 | EUR-Lex, national authorities, official sector and supplier disclosures. |
| Mitigation and monitoring | Slide 7 | NIST, ENISA, CISA, sector guidance and case-study lessons. |

## Evidence Controls

- Evidence cut-off: 10 October 2026.
- Recency ranking: 2026 material first, then H2 2025, then H1 2025.
- Older case-study material: historical only, with a stated relevance rationale.
- Source tiers: Tier 1 for regulatory statements and material facts where available; Tier 2 for transparent independent research; Tier 3 only for discovery or corroboration.
- Operating boundary: public external data only. No bank-specific control, compliance, loss, recovery, capital or risk appetite claim.
